An Azerbaijani text-to-speech voice, from corpus to API

International Bank of Azerbaijan · 2024–2026

Azerbaijani has almost no public speech data, so the voice had to be built from the ground up. I generated a 70+ hour synthetic corpus with Gemini voices across two speakers, then recorded and curated about five hours each from two in-house speakers. Training ran in two stages: fine-tune on the large synthetic set, then fine-tune that checkpoint on the real recordings.

The hard part was text, not audio. Banking text is full of abbreviations, product names and invented words (campaign names, card names) that no dictionary covers, so I built the normalization layer that turns them into something a model can say correctly.

I trained and compared small VITS, F5-TTS and Piper models, then wrote the benchmark suite that certified the winner for production: latency, time to first byte, real-time factor, audio duration, success rate and behaviour under concurrent load. The result ships as an API inside the bank's assistant and runs on CPU or GPU.

Virtual assistants that answer, and then act

International Bank of Azerbaijan · 2023–present

I own the AI layer of the bank's assistants: the customer-facing assistant, internal chatbots for employees, and an SQL assistant that turns questions into queries. They answer from documents, and they also do things: I implemented action flows such as opening a support ticket directly from the conversation, for both customers and staff.

Plain vector retrieval broke once the assistant had too many routes and dimensions to choose between. I redesigned it as a graph-based RAG workflow in LangGraph, where retrieval, routing and tool calls are explicit nodes, backed by LangChain and a Milvus vector store with cloud LLM APIs behind it.

Quality is measured, not assumed: every change is scored against a labeled evaluation set and reviewed by people before it reaches users.

CV Evaluator: requirement-by-requirement CV matching with cited evidence

Open source · 2026 · github.com/AsmarHajizada/cv-evaluator

A tool that checks a CV against a job description and marks each requirement as supported, partial or missing, citing the CV passages it used as evidence. It also rates the writing, suggests rewrites, and lists the five changes most likely to help.

The pipeline parses the CV with Docling into numbered, section-tagged passages, extracts must-have and nice-to-have requirements from the posting with an LLM, matches each one against the passages, and scores the result (must-haves weighted double). Every LLM step validates its own output: a citation to a passage that doesn't exist, a skipped requirement, or a number invented in a rewrite is caught and sent back for a retry. Job postings can be pasted or fetched from a URL via schema.org JobPosting data.

Python backend with FastAPI, PydanticAI and a CLI; Next.js front end; offline tests with a scripted model. Next step is a compact fine-tuned matcher, evaluated against a frontier LLM on agreement with human reviewers, consistency, latency and cost.
